# Periodontal Disease

Periodontitis is a chronic inflammatory disease that destroys the supporting
apparatus of the teeth, leading to attachment loss and, when untreated, tooth
loss. Staging reflects severity and complexity; grading reflects rate of
progression and risk.

Initial therapy is non-surgical: scaling and root planing with oral hygiene
instruction and risk-factor control. Smoking and poorly controlled diabetes are
major modifiable risk factors that should be addressed.

Re-evaluation occurs four to six weeks after non-surgical therapy. Sites with
residual probing depths of 5 mm or greater, especially with bleeding, may
require surgical therapy such as osseous surgery or regeneration.

Maintenance therapy at individualized intervals is essential for long-term
stability. Successful treatment is defined by reduced inflammation and probing
depths, not by regeneration alone.
