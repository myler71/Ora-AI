# Endodontic Treatment of Irreversible Pulpitis

Irreversible pulpitis is characterized by spontaneous pain, lingering thermal
sensitivity, and occasionally referred pain. When the pulp is judged
irreversibly inflamed, the accepted definitive treatment is pulpectomy or full
root canal therapy; vital pulp therapy may be considered in selected cases.

Diagnosis relies on history, thermal and percussion testing, and radiographic
evaluation. A lingering response to cold, or pain that wakes the patient,
points toward irreversible pulpitis. Percussion sensitivity may indicate
apical periodontitis in addition to pulpitis.

Chemomechanical debridement followed by three-dimensional obturation remains
the standard. Sodium hypochlorite is the primary irrigant; disinfection quality
correlates with treatment outcome. Rubber dam isolation is mandatory.

Postoperative pain is common and usually managed with analgesics. Persistent
symptoms may indicate missed canals, incomplete debridement, or fracture, and
warrant re-evaluation.
