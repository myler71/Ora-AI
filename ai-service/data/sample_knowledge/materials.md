# Restorative Materials: Composite vs Amalgam

Resin composite offers adhesion and aesthetics and permits conservative cavity
design. It is technique-sensitive, requiring moisture control; adequate
isolation improves longevity. Composite is preferred for visible surfaces.

Dental amalgam is a durable, cost-effective material with a long clinical track
record, particularly in load-bearing posterior restorations. Its use has
declined for aesthetic and environmental reasons but remains appropriate for
selected high-load or hard-to-isolate sites.

Glass-ionomer cements release fluoride and bond chemically, useful as liners,
bases, and for atraumatic restorative treatment in caries-prone patients.

Material selection should account for lesion size, load, isolation, caries
risk, and patient preference. Longevity depends more on operator technique and
occlusal factors than on material choice alone.
