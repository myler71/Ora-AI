# Caries Management

Dental caries is a biofilm-mediated, sugar-driven, multifactorial disease that
causes demineralization of tooth structure. Early lesions can remineralize when
the local environment favors mineral deposition; cavitated lesions require
operative intervention.

Risk assessment should precede treatment planning. Low-risk patients may be
managed with fluoride, sealants, and dietary counseling. High-risk patients
often require more frequent recall and additional fluoride exposure.

Restorative decisions depend on lesion extent. For occlusal and proximal
lesions confined to enamel or just beyond, minimally invasive techniques such
as resin infiltration or small composite restorations are appropriate. For
deeper lesions with pulpal involvement, indirect restorations or endodontic
referral should be considered.

Recall intervals are individualized. A three-to-six month interval is common
for high-caries-risk patients, while low-risk adults may be seen annually.
