# Medication Considerations in Dental Care

Anticoagulated patients (warfarin, direct oral anticoagulants) require care
coordination before surgical procedures. Most routine dental procedures do not
require discontinuation of anticoagulation, but invasive surgery may. The
prescribing physician should be consulted for guidance; do not independently
stop a patient's anticoagulant.

Bisphosphonates and other antiresorptive agents are associated with a risk of
medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ), particularly after
extractions in long-term or intravenous users. A thorough drug history is
essential before dentoalveolar surgery.

Antibiotic prophylaxis is reserved for specific high-risk cardiac and joint
indications per current guidelines. Routine prophylaxis for all prosthetic
patients is no longer recommended.

Patients with medication allergies must have the allergen verified and
cross-reactivity considered before any prescription is written.
